# 1. Invariant Residue

---

## Motivation

The Boundary Projection maps persistent organizational structure into
the Projective Target Manifold.

However, projection alone does not determine which aspects of the
original organization remain unchanged.

The framework therefore requires a precise mathematical definition of
what survives projection.

The Invariant Residue fulfills this role.

It identifies the organizational content preserved under admissible
projection.

---

## Formal Definition

Let

Π : ∂Ξ → M

be the Boundary Projection.

For each point

p ∈ M,

define the projection preimage

Π⁻¹(p) ⊂ ∂Ξ.

Let

G_Π

denote the admissible projection-symmetry group acting upon

Π⁻¹(p).

The Invariant Residue at p is defined by

Fₚ = Inv(Π⁻¹(p))

consisting of all residue elements invariant under the action of
G_Π.

Equivalently,

Inv(Π⁻¹(p))

is the organizational information preserved under admissible boundary
projection.

---

## Interpretation

The Invariant Residue represents what survives the projection process.

It is not the complete boundary structure itself.

Rather, it is the mathematically preserved organizational content that
remains invariant under admissible projection.

Within the MK43 Time-Space framework, this residue provides the bridge
between persistent organizational geometry and the subsequent Quantum
Field Bundle.

---

## Foundational Justification

Projection necessarily discards some information while preserving other
information.

The framework therefore distinguishes

projected organization,

from

preserved organization.

The Invariant Residue identifies exactly the preserved organizational
content.

Without this definition, the transition from projected geometry to the
Residue / Quantum Field Bundle would remain mathematically ambiguous.

---

Primitive P10 — Boundary Projection

Primitive P11 — Projective Target Manifold

---

## Remarks

The term "residue" should not be interpreted as leftover matter or
energy.

Within the MK43 Time-Space framework, residue refers exclusively to the
organizational information that remains invariant under admissible
projection.

This invariant organization becomes the mathematical foundation upon
which the Residue / Quantum Field Bundle is constructed.

---

## Definition Summary

**Name**

Invariant Residue

**Primary Symbols**

Inv, Π, ∂Ξ, M, Fₚ

**Purpose**

# 2. Admissible Equivalence

---

## Motivation

The preceding primitives establish a structured temporal manifold,
admissible continuation, inherited organizational states, chirality
organization, and chirality transport.

The framework now requires a precise criterion for determining when two
organizational states should be regarded as representing the same
underlying organization despite occurring at different stages of
admissible temporal continuation.

Without such a definition, organizational persistence cannot be stated
mathematically.

The Admissible Equivalence relation fulfills this role.

---

## Formal Definition

Let

π_E : E → T

be the Inherited State Bundle, and let

I : E → E

be the Inheritance Morphism covering the Continuation Operator

C : T → T.

For inherited organizational states

ξ₁, ξ₂ ∈ E,

define

ξ₁ ~ ξ₂

if and only if the following conditions simultaneously hold.

1. Their associated temporal states lie upon the same admissible
continuation orbit,

π_E(ξ₂) = Cⁿ(π_E(ξ₁))

for some integer

n ∈ ℤ.

2. Their chirality organization agrees under admissible chirality
transport,

χ(ξ₂) = τⁿ(χ(ξ₁)).

3. Their invariant residue agrees,

Inv(ξ₁) = Inv(ξ₂).

Thus,

ξ₁ ~ ξ₂

if and only if there exists

n ∈ ℤ

such that all three conditions above are satisfied simultaneously.

---

## Interpretation

Two inherited organizational states are considered admissibly
equivalent when they represent the same recoverable organizational
structure observed at different stages of admissible temporal
continuation.

Within the MK43 Time-Space framework, admissible equivalence compares
organizational identity rather than instantaneous temporal position.

It therefore provides the mathematical language through which persistent
organization may be discussed independently of individual continuation
steps.

---

## Foundational Justification

The framework distinguishes temporal progression from organizational
identity.

Continuation alone does not determine whether two organizational states
represent the same underlying structure.

Likewise, agreement of chirality organization alone is insufficient.

Admissible Equivalence therefore requires simultaneous agreement of

- admissible continuation,

- chirality organization, and

- invariant residue.

Together these conditions provide the minimal mathematical criterion for
organizational identity throughout admissible temporal continuation.

# 3. Projective Target Manifold

---

## Motivation

The Boundary Projection establishes the mathematical mechanism by which
persistent organizational boundaries are projected into a geometric
representation.

The framework now requires a precise definition of the geometric object
that receives this projection.

The Projective Target Manifold fulfills this role.

It defines the canonical geometric space arising from admissible
projection.

---

## Formal Definition

Let

∂Ξ

denote the Persistent Boundary.

Let

G_Π

be the admissible projection-symmetry group acting upon

∂Ξ.

The Projective Target Manifold is defined as the quotient

M = ∂Ξ / G_Π.

The canonical quotient projection is

Π : ∂Ξ → M,

where

Π(x) = [x]

denotes the equivalence class of x under the admissible projection
symmetry.

If the residue fibers are complex lines, then locally

M ≅ ℂPᵏ.

If the residue fibers are real lines, then locally

M ≅ ℝPᵏ.

Within the MK43 Time-Space framework, the quantum-field interface adopts
the complex projective case.

---

## Interpretation

The Projective Target Manifold represents projected organizational
structure after admissible projection has identified equivalent boundary
configurations.

It is not created independently.

Rather, it is inherited from the Persistent Boundary through the action
of the Boundary Projection.

The resulting manifold provides the geometric domain upon which the
Residue / Quantum Field Bundle is subsequently constructed.

---

## Foundational Justification

The framework distinguishes

persistent organizational structure,

from

its projected geometric representation.

Without this distinction, projected geometry would have no mathematically
defined origin.

The quotient construction identifies organizationally equivalent
boundary configurations while preserving the geometric information
required for subsequent bundle and field constructions.

This provides the minimal mathematical definition required for the
Projective Target Manifold.

---

Within the MK43 Time-Space framework, the Projective Target Manifold is

---

## Definition Summary

mathematical framework beginning with structured temporal organization
and concluding with geometric field configurations and observable

class of mathematical objects that may be presented to established
quantum field theory.

The Quantum-Field Input Class fulfills this role.

It identifies the admissible mathematical structures produced by the
MK43 Time-Space framework that are compatible with the standard
mathematical language of modern quantum field theory.

---

## Formal Definition

Let

**𝒬**

denote the Quantum-Field Input Class.

𝒬

a smooth differentiable manifold

M,

a smooth finite-rank complex vector bundle

π_F : F → M,

a Hermitian metric

h,

a Hermitian connection

∇,

R∇,

a smooth field section

Γ,

𝒜,

℘,

and a Quantum Action Functional

𝒮,

all satisfying the compatibility conditions established by the
preceding primitives and definitions.

Every mathematical object satisfying these conditions belongs to the
Quantum-Field Input Class

𝒬.

---

## Interpretation

The Quantum-Field Input Class is not itself a physical theory.

Rather, it specifies the mathematical class of objects that the
MK43 Time-Space framework is capable of presenting to established
quantum field theory.

It therefore defines the admissible interface between the internally
developed framework and the existing mathematical machinery of modern
quantum field theory.

---

## Foundational Justification

The framework intentionally separates its internal mathematical
development from the mathematical language inherited by quantum field
theory.

Rather than redefining quantum field theory, the framework develops its
own mathematical structures until they naturally assemble into an
admissible quantum-field input object.

The Quantum-Field Input Class formalizes this mathematical

The framework now requires a precise mathematical description of how
those observables decompose into measurable spectral components.

The Spectral Projection Family fulfills this role.

It defines the canonical family of projection operators associated with
admissible observables.

---

## Formal Definition

Let

**𝒜**

denote the Observable Algebra.

For every self-adjoint observable

O ∈ 𝒜,

let

O = ∑λ λ℘λ

denote its spectral decomposition.

Define

℘ = {℘λ}

to be the corresponding Spectral Projection Family.

Each projection operator

℘λ : Fₚ → Fₚ

projects the local field state onto the eigenspace associated with the
eigenvalue

λ.

---

## Interpretation

The Spectral Projection Family represents the complete family of
projection operators associated with admissible observables.

It does not introduce new observables.

Rather, it resolves existing observables into their measurable spectral
components.

The Spectral Projection Family therefore provides the mathematical
structure through which measurement acts upon the local fibers of the
Residue / Quantum Field Bundle.

---

## Foundational Justification

Observable quantities alone are insufficient to describe measurable
structure.

The framework additionally requires the projection operators that
resolve those observables into admissible eigenspaces.

The Spectral Projection Family provides this mathematical resolution.

It therefore serves as the minimal construction required to complete the
measurement structure of the framework.
